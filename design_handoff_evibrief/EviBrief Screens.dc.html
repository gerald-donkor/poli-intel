Policy brief|Ministry of Lands & Natural Resources|Public29 Jul 2026

Registering shade-tree tenure before the EUDR annex closes: Ghana's fastest route to compliant cocoa

Executive summary

Ghana's cocoa sector cannot demonstrate deforestation-free production while on-farm tree tenure remains unregistered. Tropenbos Ghana field data from four Cocoa Belt landscapes shows shade-tree retention rises with documented tenureTBG-24-017. We recommend the Ministry lay a fast-track registration instrument before the EUDR implementing annex closes on 21 August.

Evidence

Across 612 surveyed farms in Ahafo Ano and Asunafo, registered tenure correlated with a 2.4× higher rate of shade-tree retention over five yearsTBG-24-017. Comparable community-forestry registers in DR Congo produced measurable gains within two seasonsTBI-DRC-22.

Only 12% of surveyed farmers could name the legal owner of the shade trees standing on their own plots.

Tropenbos Ghana, Tree Tenure & Shade Retention in the Cocoa Belt, 2024 · p.31

Farmer-reported uptake of on-farm planting reached 38% of cocoa plots in the Western North regionunverified following the 2023 seedling programme.

Recommendations
01
Lay a fast-track shade-tree registration instrument before 26 February, using the existing Lands Commission farm register as the base layer.
02
Direct Cocobod's technical committee to align its scheme review with the registration instrument rather than run a parallel process.
